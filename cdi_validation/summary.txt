CDI VALIDATION ANALYSIS - HOW IT WORKS
========================================

WHAT THIS ANALYSIS DOES
------------------------
This checks whether the project's CHILDES-corpus-derived "age of production"
estimates for function words agree with an independent benchmark: the
MacArthur-Bates CDI (Communicative Development Inventory), as hosted on
Wordbank. Two completely different methods try to answer the same question
("when do children start producing word X?") using different data and
different statistics. This analysis puts them side by side.

Files:
  cdi_validation.Rmd              - the analysis script (knit this to rerun)
  cdi_childes_comparison.csv      - output: joined table of both estimates
  cdi_validation_plot.png         - output: scatter plot of the comparison
  summary.txt                     - this file


THE TWO DATA SOURCES
---------------------

1) CHILDES side: processed_data/age_of_production.RDS
   - Built in processing/englishPlotsAndCurves_refactored.Rmd (and the older
     processing/englishPlotsAndCurves.Rmd).
   - For each word, all child-speech tokens from CHILDES transcripts are
     counted per age (in months), turned into a CUMULATIVE relative
     frequency curve (parts per thousand of all child words at that age
     that are this word, accumulated over age).
   - A Bayesian nonlinear growth curve (Gompertz function) is fit to that
     cumulative curve per word using brms:
         cumulative_ppt ~ upperAsymptote * exp(-exp(((growthRate*e)/upperAsymptote)*(lag-age)+1))
   - The "lag" parameter of that curve is the estimated inflection point of
     the S-shaped growth curve - this is what's stored as `Estimate` (with
     `Est.Error`, `Q2.5`, `Q97.5` as its posterior SD and 95% credible
     interval). Units: age in months.
   - Interpretation: this is NOT "the age a word first appears in transcripts."
     It's a smoothed estimate of where the word's USAGE RATE starts climbing
     steeply, based on pooling all children's transcripts together.
   - Note: there are two different age_of_production.RDS files in the repo
     (processing/ and processed_data/) with non-overlapping word lists -
     both are stale, hand-saved snapshots from different runs of an evolving
     word list. This analysis uses processed_data/age_of_production.RDS
     (60 words, chosen because it includes pronouns/auxiliaries like
     I/you/he/am, which the CDI list also has - better overlap for
     comparison). It had been deleted from the working tree and was
     restored via `git checkout HEAD -- processed_data/age_of_production.RDS`
     before running this analysis.

2) CDI side: processed_data/cdi_functionwords.csv
   - Downloaded from Wordbank (2025-01-25) via the wordbankr R package, for
     English (American), the WS ("Words & Sentences") form, "produces"
     measure, ages 16-30 months.
   - For each word x age, `proportion` = the fraction of children in the
     Wordbank norming sample whose PARENT reported (via checklist) that the
     child produces that word.
   - This analysis computes "CDI onset age" per word as the first age at
     which that proportion crosses a threshold - done at three thresholds
     (0.10, 0.15, 0.20) to give both a point estimate (0.15) and a rough
     "confidence range" (0.10-0.20) for the plot. This mirrors the exact
     method already used in processing/MBCDIfunctionWords.Rmd.
   - Interpretation: this is the age at which a meaningful minority of
     surveyed children are reported to say the word at all, per parent
     checklist recall - a coarser, threshold-based, self-report measure.


WHY THE TWO METHODS AREN'T DIRECTLY COMPARABLE (BY DESIGN)
-------------------------------------------------------------
- CHILDES `Estimate` = inflection point of a continuous usage-frequency
  curve, fit from actual transcribed speech. Sensitive to even occasional
  early uses of a word.
- CDI `age_above_0.15` = age at which >=15% of parents check "yes, produces"
  on a survey. Requires the word to be established enough that a substantial
  fraction of *other* children are also saying it, and depends on parents
  noticing/recalling/reporting production accurately.
- Because of this, CHILDES estimates are expected to run systematically
  EARLIER than CDI estimates, since natural speech can pick up sparse early
  usage that hasn't yet become common enough for 15% of parents to report it.
  This is exactly what we found (see RESULTS below).


HOW THE ANALYSIS SCRIPT WORKS, STEP BY STEP
---------------------------------------------
1. Load both datasets (readRDS / read_csv).
2. Recompute CDI_onset_age from the raw proportions (age_above_0.1/.15/.2
   per word), same logic as the existing MBCDIfunctionWords.Rmd script.
3. Normalize word spelling so the two datasets can be joined on a shared
   `word` column. Only two unambiguous renames are applied:
     "can (auxiliary)" -> "can"
     "inside/in"        -> "in"
   Contractions are deliberately NOT force-matched (see LIMITATIONS below).
4. Inner join on `word`: only words present in BOTH datasets survive. This
   produced 45 matched words out of 60 CHILDES words / 86 CDI words.
   Unmatched words on each side are printed by the script for transparency
   (e.g. CHILDES-only: n't, 'm, sometimes, need, except, very, just, here,
   out, down, from, does, do, or - mostly words missing from the CDI list,
   or contractions written differently in each source).
5. Compute agreement statistics between CHILDES `Estimate` and each of the
   three CDI thresholds: Pearson correlation, Spearman correlation (rank-
   based, robust to nonlinearity), mean absolute difference, RMSE, and a
   simple linear regression (Estimate ~ age_above_0.15) for R-squared/slope.
6. Plot: x-axis = CDI age_above_0.15 (with horizontal whiskers spanning the
   0.10-0.20 threshold range), y-axis = CHILDES Estimate (with vertical
   whiskers = 95% credible interval), a dashed diagonal line marking
   "perfect agreement" (y = x), and every point labeled with its word.
7. Save the joined table and the plot to disk.


RESULTS (as of this run)
--------------------------
- 45 words matched and compared.
- Pearson r ~ 0.58-0.59, Spearman rho ~ 0.57-0.58 across all three CDI
  thresholds, all p < 0.0001. Moderate, highly significant positive
  correlation - the two methods broadly agree on which words come earlier
  vs. later, but don't line up precisely word-for-word.
- Linear regression of Estimate on age_above_0.15: R-squared ~ 0.34, slope
  ~ 0.53 (i.e. CDI age only explains about a third of the variance in the
  CHILDES estimate).
- Systematic offset: CHILDES estimates run about 3.4-5.4 months EARLIER than
  CDI on average (mean absolute difference), and the gap widens at higher
  CDI thresholds - consistent with the expected direction of bias described
  above.
- Biggest individual disagreements (CHILDES much earlier than CDI):
  about, those, all, could, our, another, who, they - each 7.5-13.5 months
  earlier by the CHILDES estimate. These are candidates worth a closer
  look (e.g. checking if a handful of early corpus tokens for these words
  are driving an artificially early growth-curve fit).


LIMITATIONS / CAVEATS
-----------------------
- Ground-truth file staleness: processed_data/age_of_production.RDS is a
  saved snapshot from an earlier run of the pipeline, not necessarily
  reflecting the current/complete word_params list in
  englishPlotsAndCurves_refactored.Rmd. If that script is re-run and
  re-saved, this comparison should be redone against the fresh output.
- Word coverage is incomplete: 45 of 60 CHILDES words matched; several
  high-frequency words (do, does, n't, 'm, or) were excluded rather than
  fuzzy-matched, because their CDI counterparts are contractions or
  differently-scoped words that would need a judgment call to equate
  correctly (e.g. is CHILDES "n't" comparable to CDI "don't", which also
  includes the "don't" verb+negation as a whole word, not just the
  contracted negative morpheme?).
- Small sample: correlation/regression stats are based on only 45 data
  points, so treat R-squared/p-values as indicative, not definitive.
- Different populations: CHILDES corpora and the Wordbank CDI norming
  sample are not the same children, same time periods, or necessarily
  matched on dialect/SES - some of the disagreement could reflect sample
  differences rather than a flaw in either method.
- CDI threshold choice affects results slightly: the three thresholds
  (0.1/0.15/0.2) give similar but not identical correlation figures; 0.15
  was used as the primary point estimate to match prior in-repo convention.


ANTICIPATED QUESTIONS
------------------------
Q: Why not just use age of FIRST occurrence in CHILDES instead of a fitted
   growth curve?
A: First occurrence is very noisy (one transcription error or one child's
   early attempt can make a word look acquired at age 12mo). The Gompertz
   fit smooths over all children/ages to find where usage robustly
   increases, which is why the project uses it - but it also means it's
   not directly the same construct as "first time observed."

Q: Why is CHILDES earlier than CDI instead of the same or later?
A: See "WHY THE TWO METHODS AREN'T DIRECTLY COMPARABLE" above - CHILDES
   can register a word from sparse early production, while CDI requires a
   noticeable fraction of parents to report the word as an established part
   of the child's vocabulary.

Q: Is a Pearson r of ~0.58 "good"?
A: For two independently-collected, methodologically different measures of
   the same underlying construct (word acquisition timing), a moderate,
   highly significant correlation is a reasonable validation result. It
   shows real signal/agreement, not proof of interchangeability - it would
   be surprising (and slightly suspicious) if r were much higher than this
   given how differently the two are computed.

Q: Can I rerun this with different words, thresholds, or a fresher
   CHILDES dataset?
A: Yes - edit cdi_validation.Rmd: change the threshold columns used in the
   stats/plot chunks, add more normalization rules in the `recode()` call
   to include more words, or point the readRDS() call at a freshly
   regenerated age_of_production.RDS once the main pipeline is rerun.

Q: Where do I look if I want to investigate the outlier words further?
A: cdi_childes_comparison.csv has both estimates and their intervals per
   word - filter/sort by the difference between Estimate and
   age_above_0.15. For deeper investigation of a specific word's CHILDES
   growth curve, look at the corresponding *_lagModel fit and per-word plot
   in processing/englishPlotsAndCurves_refactored.Rmd or the modeling/
   directory (cached brms model files).
